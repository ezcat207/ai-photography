# Chapter 0: A Wall of Problems

First day of class, no textbooks. Just look at the wall.

Everything pinned here is a real face-plant — some shot on cameras, some generated by AI. Laugh first, then walk into Chapter 1 with questions. Every chapter of this book is here to tear one brick off this wall.

![A wall of problems](images/ch00-wall.webp)

## 10 problems on the wall

**1. "Why does the AI never draw what I want?"**
You see it clearly in your head, but what comes out is something else. Root cause: you haven't learned to translate "vibes" into decisions. Chapter 1 fixes it.

**2. "I said 'dusk,' it gave me noon"**
The AI isn't messing with you — you just didn't say anything about light. Chapter 8 fixes it.

**3. "Why does the hand have six fingers?"**
AI is famously terrible at hands. Cause + rescue plan: Chapter 10.

**4. "The sign text is total gibberish"**
AI can't read. Don't ask it to. Chapter 10.

**5. "I wanted anime, it gave me a photo"**
You never named a style, so the AI picked a mood. Chapter 1 hands you the "style" knob.

**6. "How do I even say 'blurred background' to an AI?"**
Photographers call it "wide aperture, shallow depth of field." To an AI just say "blurred background, sharp subject." Chapter 2.

**7. "Does the AI understand backlight and side light?"**
Yes — better than you'd think. Chapter 8.

**8. "What do aperture and shutter have to do with AI?"**
Everything: the 8 decisions a photographer twists by hand, you say out loud. Chapter 1 covers all eight at once.

**9. "I want the photo warmer — what words do I use?"**
"Warm and nostalgic" — four syllables, done. Chapter 9.

**10. "I wrote a whole paragraph of prompt and the AI ignored all of it"**
The more words, the dizzier the AI. Important stuff first, three lines max. Chapter 10.

## How to use this wall

Finish a chapter, tear off a brick. When all 10 are gone, you've climbed from Bronze to Gold.

One more rule in this book: every concept gets tested on the **selfie** — the thing you shoot most is the best lab rat. Chapter 1 gives you the map first: a photo is just counting photons, and the only characters are light, the lens, and you.
