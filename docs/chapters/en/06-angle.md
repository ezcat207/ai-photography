# Chapter 6: Camera Angle — Crouch or Hold High

**Map position**: You · where you stand.

Same legs — why does she look 5'11" and you look 4'11"? Not the legs. The camera position. This chapter is selfie core tech.

![Extreme low angle: teen on steps looks tall and powerful](images/ch06-angle.webp)

Look at this shot: camera basically on the ground, looking up — the kid on the steps looks like a giant. He didn't grow. The angle is "lying," benevolently.

## What angle is

**One sentence**: how high the camera sits and which way it tilts — it decides whether a person looks tall or short, cool or cute.

**In plain English** — four moves:

- **Low angle, looking up**: taller, cooler, more powerful. Phone at ankle height, shoot up — every "long legs" post on your feed was born this way. But don't shoot faces from below (double chin says hi) — full body is safest.
- **High angle, looking down**: smaller face. Your raised-arm selfie exists because of geometry. But full body gets squished — cute, not tall.
- **Eye level**: most honest. Friends talking, staring down your cat.
- **Tilted (dutch angle)**: frame crooked on purpose — edgy, rebellious, skate-street energy. Keep it 5–10 degrees.

**Two cold facts**:
1. Shooting kids or pets? Crouch to their eye level. Adults shooting down at kids = all "big head, tiny body."
2. Shooting gear — sneakers, skateboards — ground-level looking up gives instant ad energy.

### Selfie lab

45 degrees up, face a touch smaller — the universal selfie pose on Earth, and it's geometry, not vanity. Full-body looking tall? Phone at ankle height, 3-second timer, shoot up. Angle is free plastic surgery; geometry is the free doctor.

## Translating to prompts

- Tall and powerful: "low angle shot looking up, towering figure"
- Small face: "high angle looking down, delicate face"
- Edgy: "dutch angle, tilted composition, street energy"

## Experiment you can redo

Same pair of sneakers, three angles: ground-level up / crouch eye-level / held-high down. Which pair looks most expensive? Angle is the cheapest filter ever made.

## Blooper warnings

- **Double-chin blooper**: low angle on a face — the chin surrenders first. Low angles for full body, high angles for faces.
- **Too-tilted blooper**: dutch past 15 degrees and viewers think your hands shake. Small tilt = style, big tilt = accident.
