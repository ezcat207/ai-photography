# Chapter 2: Aperture — Blurred Backgrounds

**Map position**: Lens · how wide the faucet opens.

Why do influencers' selfies have creamy blurred backgrounds while yours have strangers photobombing? It's not the face — it's aperture. Portrait Mode on vs. off is half your likes.

![Teen girl with bubble tea, background melted into bokeh](images/ch02-aperture.webp)

Look at this shot: her face tack sharp, the milk-tea shop melted into soft glowing orbs behind her. Your eyes don't even want to look anywhere else — that's aperture directing traffic.

## What aperture is

**One sentence**: the "pupil" inside the lens. It controls two things — how much light gets in, and how blurry the background goes.

**In plain English**: counterintuitive but memorize it — **smaller number = bigger opening**. f/1.8 is "eyes wide open," f/16 is "squinting."

- **f/1.8–f/2.8 (wide open)**: background fully melted, subject pops. Portraits, bubble tea, cats — use this.
- **f/8–f/11 (narrow)**: sharp front to back. Group photos, landscapes, buildings — use this.
- **f/4–f/5.6 (middle)**: everyday mode, roughly what your phone does by default.

**Three cold facts**:
1. Portrait Mode is just *pretending* to be a wide aperture — it's computed, not optical, which is why hair edges glitch sometimes.
2. Wide open, the sharp zone is paper-thin: nose sharp, ears already soft. Don't get greedy — f/2 is the sweet spot for people.
3. Group photo at f/1.8 with 30 people = the back row turns to ghosts. Classic face-plant, on trial again in Chapter 10.

### Selfie lab

Portrait Mode on for selfies: your messy room melts away, all eyes on your face — aperture's most direct social currency. Two costs to remember: hair edges glitch (it's math, not glass), and don't go too wide — f/2-ish looks most human.

## Translating to prompts

Talk to AI like a human, no f-numbers needed:

- Want blur: "shallow depth of field, background melted into soft bokeh, sharp subject"
- Want everything sharp: "deep depth of field, sharp front to back"
- Want this vibe: "window seat, bokeh lights in background, cozy"

## Experiment you can redo

Same cup of bubble tea, two shots: Portrait Mode on (fake f/1.8) vs. regular (fake f/8). Compare the background: glowing orbs vs. strangers. Fifteen minutes and you'll never forget aperture.

## Blooper warnings

- **Greed blooper**: f/1.4 on a face — eyes sharp, nose tip soft. Aperture isn't "bigger is better." Stop down one click.
- **Group blooper**: wide aperture + many people = ghost back row. More people, bigger f-number.
