# Chapter 7: Composition — Where Things Go

**Map position**: You · how you arrange.

Same spot — why does their IG look like a magazine and yours look like a tourist brochure? Not the spot. The arrangement. Selfies included: stop dead-centering your face.

![Old stone doorway framing a teen with a skateboard](../images/ch07-composition.webp)

Look at this shot: a kid with a skateboard inside an old stone doorway, cobblestone lines walking from the foreground straight to him. Three composition moves in one frame — framing, leading lines, rule of thirds. Of the 8 decisions, this one costs nothing. Your phone can practice it today.

## What composition is

**One sentence**: placement inside the frame — it decides where eyes go first, then where.

**In plain English** — five moves for life:

- **Rule of thirds**: one rule — stop centering people. Grid on, face on an intersection, instantly better.
- **Leading lines**: roads, railings, stairs pull eyes to the subject like quest markers in a game.
- **Framing**: doorways, windows, tree holes as picture frames — instant story.
- **Negative space**: don't stuff the frame. Sky, walls, water give the subject "breathing room."
- **Symmetry**: buildings, reflections — deeply satisfying to the orderly brain.

**Two cold facts**:
1. Composition's first principle is subtraction: every extra thing in frame steals a slice of attention. Before shooting, ask: anything here that doesn't need to be?
2. Tilted horizon kills even genius composition — grid on, one glance before you shoot.

### Selfie lab

Grid on. Eyes on the upper-third intersection. One fist of headroom. And check: no lamppost "growing" out of your head. That's the entire difference between influencer selfies and dad selfies.

## Translating to prompts

- Thirds: "rule of thirds, subject on the right intersection"
- Lines: "cobblestone leading lines pointing at the subject"
- Frame: "doorway as a picture frame, frame-within-frame"
- Space: "generous negative space, minimal"
- Symmetry: "symmetrical composition, reflection"

## Experiment you can redo

Same street, two shots: one centered with everything crammed in, one rule-of-thirds with a single person. Post both. Let the likes teach you composition.

## Blooper warnings

- **Tourist blooper**: too many elements — the building AND the person AND the sign. One photo, one story.
- **Horizon blooper**: the sea is diagonal, the tower leans. Grid on. Three seconds.
