# Chapter 3: Shutter — Frozen vs. Flowing

**Map position**: Lens · how long the faucet stays open.

You jump for a selfie, trying to freeze the peak — and get a blurry ghost. You didn't jump too low. Your "blink" was too slow.

![Water balloon bursting, splash frozen into a crown](../images/ch03-shutter.webp)

Look at this shot: a water balloon popping, the splash frozen into a crown. In real life that moment lives for 1/8000 of a second — the shutter stole it and nailed it to the photo.

## What shutter is

**One sentence**: the camera's "blink speed." It decides whether time freezes or flows.

**In plain English**:

- **Fast blink (1/500s and up)**: time frozen. Splashes, dunks, jumping cats, kickflips mid-air — all live here. Shooting anything that moves? Set 1/500 first, ask questions later.
- **Normal blink (1/60–1/250s)**: everyday speed. Below 1/60, shaky hands = blurry photo. That's where "safe shutter speed" comes from.
- **Slow blink (1 second or more)**: time flows. Night traffic smears into light ribbons, waterfalls turn to silk, stars draw circles. Tripod (or a very steady table) required — handheld is doomed.

**Two cold facts**:
1. Video has shutter too, but that's a later book's problem.
2. Slow shutter + flash = "frozen subject, flowing background" — concert photographers live on this trick. Just know it exists for now.

### Selfie lab

Jumping selfies, kickflip selfies — if the shutter (burst) can't keep up, it's ghost town. Use burst mode: 10 shots per jump, one of them catches the peak. That's shutter's social currency, delivered.

## Translating to prompts

- Want frozen: "frozen mid-air, splash suspended, tack sharp"
- Want flowing: "long exposure, traffic smeared into light trails" / "slow shutter, waterfall like silk"
- Want speed: "motion blur, sense of speed"

## Experiment you can redo

Open a faucet. Two shots: sports mode (fast) vs. night mode (slow). Look at the water: individual droplets vs. a silk ribbon. Shutter, understood in 3 minutes.

## Blooper warnings

- **Shaky blooper**: slower than 1/60 handheld — no one can save it. Lean on a wall, rest the phone on a table, or burst.
- **Blurry-kid blooper**: shooting kids or pets slower than 1/500 and blaming them for moving too much. It's not them. It's your blink.
