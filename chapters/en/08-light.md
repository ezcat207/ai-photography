# Chapter 8: Light — Where the Sun Stands

**Map position**: Light · from where.

Same girl — noon shot looks like an ID photo, dusk shot looks like a movie lead. Not the girl. Where the sun stood. Free plastic surgery, called lighting.

![Sunset backlight: girl's hair rimmed in gold](../images/ch08-light.webp)

Look at this shot: a girl on a rooftop at sunset, the sun behind her, her hair rimmed in gold. That's backlight — this chapter's most romantic move.

## What light is

**One sentence**: which direction light comes from, how hard or soft — it decides depth and mood.

**In plain English** — three directions first:

- **Front light** (sun behind you): ID-photo energy. Clear but flat, zero depth.
- **Side light** (sun to the side): half the face lit, half in shadow — maximum depth. The "cool guy" look, great for food texture too.
- **Backlight** (sun across from you): silhouette, but hair and outlines catch fire — "main-character aura" comes from here.

Then two times of day:

- **Golden hour** (the hour around sunrise/sunset): honey-colored light, everything looks good. Schedule shoots then.
- **Blue hour** (20 minutes after sunset): deep blue sky, streetlights just on — the city at its most storied.

**Two cold facts**:
1. Noon overhead sun = panda eyes: sun on top, eye sockets go black. Don't shoot people at noon — shoot buildings instead (short shadows, crisp edges).
2. Overcast sky is a free softbox: clouds scatter the sun. Friendliest possible portrait light — smooth skin, no angle-hunting.

### Selfie lab

Window light is the best selfie light in existence. Sit by a window in daytime — soft light wraps your face, no smoothing app needed. Never selfie under a ceiling light. That's a panda-eye machine. Want "vibes"? Golden hour, backlit selfie, hair on fire.

## Translating to prompts

- Blue hour: "blue hour, deep blue sky, streetlights just on"

## Experiment you can redo

Same friend, two shots: noon vs. 6pm. No editing, just compare — the lighting chapter graduates in 10 minutes.

## Blooper warnings

- **Panda blooper**: overhead noon sun on a face — eye sockets turn to caves. Change the time, or find tree shade.
- **Black-face blooper**: backlight with no fill = face gone. Either commit to the silhouette (honestly beautiful) or have a friend bounce phone light from the front.
